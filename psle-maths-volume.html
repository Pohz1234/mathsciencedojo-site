PSLE Maths Volume of Cuboids and Water Tanks: Litres and Immersed Objects

Volume is the space a solid takes up, and 1 litre is the same as 1000 cm³. Most marks are lost in water-tank questions, when students forget to change units or ignore that water cannot rise above the top of the tank.

Volume in one step chain

  • 1. Draw and label. Sketch the tank or solid and write every length in the same unit (cm is safest).
  • 2. Base area first. Length × breadth gives the base area in cm².
  • 3. Multiply by height. Base area × height gives the volume in cm³. For water, use the water height, not the tank height.
  • 4. Convert. 1000 cm³ = 1 litre. Change only at the end, or when the question asks.
  • 5. Answer with the unit: cm³ for volume, litres for water, cm for a height.

The facts to know

  • Cuboid: volume = length × breadth × height = base area × height.
  • Cube: volume = side × side × side.
  • Units: 1 ml = 1 cm³. 1 litre = 1000 ml = 1000 cm³.
  • Missing height of water: height = volume ÷ base area.
  • Object in water: volume of object = base area of the tank × rise in water level (when the object is fully under the water and nothing spills).
  • Capacity is the most water a container can hold. It is the volume of the container.

Worked examples

Example 1: litres of water in a tank

A tank is 40 cm long, 25 cm wide and 30 cm tall. It contains water to a depth of 18 cm. How many litres of water are in the tank?

Base area = 40 × 25 = 1000 cm²
Volume of water = 1000 × 18 = 18 000 cm³
18 000 ÷ 1000 = 18
There are 18 litres of water. (The 30 cm tank height is not used, because the water only reaches 18 cm.)

Example 2: volume of a stone

A tank has a base of 50 cm by 30 cm. The water is 12 cm deep. A stone is lowered into the water and is completely under the water. The water level rises to 15 cm. Find the volume of the stone.

Rise in water level = 15 − 12 = 3 cm
Volume of stone = 50 × 30 × 3 = 4500
The volume of the stone is 4500 cm³. (The water that moved up has the same volume as the stone.)

Tricky question: when the water overflows

Practice question

A rectangular tank is 40 cm long, 25 cm wide and 30 cm tall. It contains water to a depth of 27 cm. A solid metal block measuring 25 cm by 20 cm by 10 cm is placed in the tank and is completely under the water. (a) How much water overflows? Give your answer in litres. (b) How many litres of water are left in the tank?

Worked solution
Label: base area of the tank = 40 × 25 = 1000 cm².
(a) Find the space left, then compare:
Volume of block = 25 × 20 × 10 = 5000 cm³
Space between the water and the top of the tank = 30 − 27 = 3 cm
Volume of this space = 1000 × 3 = 3000 cm³
The block takes up 5000 cm³ but only 3000 cm³ of space is free.
Overflow = 5000 − 3000 = 2000 cm³ = 2 litres
2 litres of water overflow.
(b) The tank is now full of block and water:
Volume of tank = 1000 × 30 = 30 000 cm³
Water left = 30 000 − 5000 = 25 000 cm³ = 25 litres
25 litres of water are left.
Check: water at the start = 1000 × 27 = 27 000 cm³. Take away the 2000 cm³ that overflowed: 25 000 cm³. It matches.

Why it is tricky: many students say the water rises by 5 cm (5000 ÷ 1000) and go to 32 cm, which is taller than the tank. Water cannot rise above the top, so the extra spills out. Always compare the rise with the space left.

Mistakes that cost marks

  • Using the tank height instead of the depth of the water.
  • Forgetting that 1 litre is 1000 cm³, or dividing by 100 instead of 1000.
  • Mixing m and cm in the same multiplication.
  • Letting the water rise above the top of the tank instead of finding the overflow.
  • Writing cm² or cm for volume. Volume is in cm³.
  • Not showing the base area first, which loses the method mark when the final number is wrong.

Volume questions

How do you find the volume of a cuboid? +

Volume = length × breadth × height, in cm³. You can also find the base area first, then multiply by the height. A cube is a cuboid with all sides equal, so its volume is side × side × side.

How many ml are in 1 litre, and how many cm³? +

1 litre = 1000 ml = 1000 cm³. So 1 ml = 1 cm³. This link is how you change between volume of a tank and litres of water.

How do you find the volume of an object put into water? +

Fully immerse the object. The water level rises, and the volume of the object equals the volume of water that rose: base area of the tank × rise in water level. If the water spills over, the object's volume is the rise plus the overflow.

How do you find the height of water when the volume is given? +

Height = volume ÷ base area. Change litres to cm³ first, then divide by the base area of the tank in cm².

Why does my child lose marks in volume questions? +

The usual causes are mixing units (cm, m, litres), using the whole tank height instead of the water height, forgetting that water cannot rise above the tank, and writing cm² or cm instead of cm³.
