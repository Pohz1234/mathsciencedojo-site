Why Capable Kids Lose Marks in PSLE Science Open-Ended Questions

Your child studies and understands the topic, yet Section B still takes 8–10 marks off them. Here are the five answering leaks behind it, and the fix for each.

Short answer: most marks lost in PSLE Science open-ended questions come from how the answer is written, not from what the child knows. The five leaks are missing keywords, explaining instead of applying, half a cause-and-effect chain, missing experiment variables, and rushing. Each is fixable with a fixed answer structure.

I am Mr Yeo, a teacher and the founder of MathScienceDojo, and I teach PSLE Science to P5 and P6 students in small live online classes. After years of marking primary science scripts, the pattern is consistent: the child knew the science, but the answer was not written in a way the marking scheme could reward.

1. Missing keywords: a loose paraphrase scores zero

PSLE Science open-ended answers are marked against specific keywords and phrases. A student can describe a concept perfectly in their own words and still miss the mark, because the marker is looking for terms like "gain/lose heat", "water vapour condenses", or "less water is lost through the leaves".

The fix: learn the keyword pattern topic by topic. For every common question type there is a reliable answer structure, and once a student has it, the same 2 marks stop leaking on every paper.

2. Explaining the concept instead of answering the question

A question asks "Explain why the water level in the container decreased." The student writes the definition of evaporation, the factors, the water cycle. The missing piece is the link back to this container, this water level. Markers award marks for application, not recitation.

The fix: every answer names the objects in the question, and ends with a phrase that ties the science back to the exact scenario.

3. Stating only half of the cause-and-effect chain

Two-mark questions usually want a complete chain: what happened, and why. Students often give one half, "the ice melted", without the other, "because it gained heat from the surroundings." Half a chain earns half the marks at best.

The fix: the "because → therefore" check. Before moving on, ask: did I state the cause AND the effect? A 2-mark question with a one-clause answer is missing something.

4. Experiment questions answered without the variables

Questions about aims, fair tests and reliability trip up strong students. An answer about the aim must mention the changed variable and the measured variable. An answer about reliability must mention repeating the experiment and averaging the results.

The fix: memorise three experiment answer frames: aim, fair test, reliability. They are almost fill-in-the-blank once learned.

5. Rushing at the end of the paper

Section B sits at the end of the paper, when students are tired. Answers shrink to fragments, keywords get skipped and questions get misread. A student who would score 4/5 on a topic at home can score 2/5 in the hall.

The fix: timed drills under real conditions, with a fixed time budget per question. Speed with structure is trainable.

Worked example: the same answer, before and after

Question (2 marks): A beaker of water was left uncovered on a table for three days. The water level dropped. Explain why.

A typical answer that loses marks: "The water dried up because it was hot."
No keyword, no mechanism, no link to the water level.

A full-marks answer: "The water at the surface gained heat from the surroundings and evaporated into water vapour, which escaped into the air. Less water remained in the beaker, so the water level dropped."

Why it scores: it uses the keywords (gained heat, evaporated, water vapour), states the cause and the effect, and ends on the water level in the beaker.

The same structure works for topics like Heat, where the keywords are good and poor conductor of heat. To practise it on your own questions, try the free Study Buddy.

The pattern behind all five: these are answering-technique problems, not intelligence problems. That is why scores can move fast once the technique is fixed. My students have gone from 35% to 88% in four months without becoming "smarter." They simply stopped giving marks away.

Questions parents ask

Why does my child lose marks in PSLE Science when they know the concepts? +

Because open-ended questions are marked on whether the answer contains the expected scientific keywords and links the cause to the effect in the scenario given. A child can understand a topic and still write a loose, everyday-language answer that the marking scheme cannot reward.

What are the most common mistakes in PSLE Science open-ended questions? +

Leaving out the marking keywords, explaining the concept without tying it to the object in the question, stating only half of a cause-and-effect chain, missing the variables in experiment questions, and rushing answers under time pressure.

How do I answer a 2-mark PSLE Science explain question? +

Give two linked points: the scientific reason using the correct keyword, and the effect on the specific object or situation in the question. For example, say that the water gained heat and evaporated into water vapour, so less water remained and the water level dropped.

When should my child start practising PSLE Science answering technique? +

From Primary 5 is a sensible start, so the habits are built before the P6 year. A child who already understands the topics but loses marks in Section B benefits most from focused technique practice.
