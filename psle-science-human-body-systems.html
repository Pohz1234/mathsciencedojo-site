PSLE Science Human Body Systems: Breathing and Blood Circulation Explained

Blood is the delivery van: it picks up oxygen at the lungs and digested food at the small intestine, and drops them off at every body part. Most marks are lost by writing that we breathe in only oxygen, or by not linking the lungs to the blood.

Breathing brings oxygen to the lungs; blood carries it to the body

Remember it as a step chain: air → nose or mouth → windpipe → lungs → oxygen passes into the blood → heart pumps the blood → body parts. At the lungs, oxygen passes from the air into the blood and carbon dioxide passes from the blood into the air, which is then breathed out.

  • Respiratory system: nose, windpipe and lungs. Its job is to take in air and let gases move between the air and the blood.
  • Circulatory system: heart, blood vessels and blood. Its job is to move blood around the whole body.
  • Heart: a muscular pump. It pumps blood to the lungs and to the rest of the body.
  • Blood vessels: tubes that carry the blood. The smallest ones reach every part of the body.

Breathed-in air and breathed-out air are different

Step chain: oxygen passes into the blood → less oxygen left in the air, and carbon dioxide passes out of the blood → more carbon dioxide in the air. So compared with breathed-in air, breathed-out air has less oxygen and more carbon dioxide. It also has more water vapour, which is why a mirror mists up when you breathe on it.

Blood is a delivery system: it carries three things

  • Oxygen: picked up at the lungs and delivered to all body parts.
  • Digested food: absorbed into the blood in the small intestine and delivered to all body parts. This links the digestive system to the circulatory system.
  • Carbon dioxide (waste): collected from body parts and carried to the lungs to be breathed out.

Why the body needs them: the cells of the body use oxygen and digested food to release energy. Carbon dioxide is made in this process and must be removed.

Good to know: blood has parts with different jobs. Red blood cells carry oxygen and white blood cells help to fight germs.

Exercise: more energy needed → more oxygen needed → faster breathing and heartbeat

Step chain: muscles work harder → need more energy → need more oxygen and digested food → breathing rate and heartbeat increase → more oxygen taken in and blood delivers it faster. Carbon dioxide is also produced faster, so it is removed faster.

Common student mistakes

  • Writing that we breathe in only oxygen and breathe out only carbon dioxide. Air is a mixture of gases, and breathed-out air still has some oxygen.
  • Writing that the lungs "make" oxygen. The lungs do not make it; oxygen passes from the air into the blood at the lungs.
  • Writing that the blood carries "air". The blood carries oxygen, digested food and carbon dioxide.
  • Writing that the heart "makes" blood. The heart pumps blood.
  • Writing that the heartbeat and breathing rate increase "because the person is tired". The keyword reason is that the muscles need more oxygen and digested food to release more energy.
  • Forgetting the link between systems: digested food gets to the body parts only because the circulatory system carries it from the small intestine.

Tricky practice question with full worked solution

Blood going to and from the lungs

Blood vessel P carries blood from the heart to the lungs. Blood vessel Q carries blood from the lungs back to the heart.

(a) Compare the amount of oxygen and the amount of carbon dioxide in the blood in P and in Q. Explain the difference.

(b) Ali runs quickly for five minutes. State what happens to his heartbeat and explain why this helps his leg muscles.

Solution.

(a) The blood in P has less oxygen and more carbon dioxide than the blood in Q. The blood in P has just come back from the body, where oxygen was delivered to body parts and carbon dioxide was collected. At the lungs, oxygen passes from the air into the blood and carbon dioxide passes from the blood into the air. So the blood in Q has more oxygen and less carbon dioxide.

(b) His heartbeat increases. His leg muscles need more energy, so they need more oxygen and digested food. A faster heartbeat pumps blood faster, so the blood delivers more oxygen and digested food to the leg muscles and carries away the carbon dioxide made there.

Why this is tricky: students swap P and Q, or only write "P has more carbon dioxide" without explaining why. Follow the blood: body → heart → lungs (P) → heart (Q) → body. Blood that has just left the body has given up oxygen; blood that has just left the lungs has gained it.

Human body systems questions

What is the job of the respiratory system in PSLE Science? +

The respiratory system brings air into the body so that oxygen can pass from the air in the lungs into the blood, and carbon dioxide can pass from the blood into the air in the lungs to be breathed out.

What does the circulatory system carry around the body? +

The blood, pumped by the heart through the blood vessels, carries oxygen, digested food and carbon dioxide (a waste gas). It delivers oxygen and digested food to all parts of the body and brings carbon dioxide back to the lungs to be removed.

Why do the breathing rate and pulse rate increase during exercise? +

The muscles work harder and need more energy, so their cells need more oxygen and digested food. The breathing rate and heartbeat increase so that more oxygen is taken in and the blood delivers more oxygen and digested food to the muscles faster. More carbon dioxide is also produced and has to be removed.

Is the air we breathe out free of oxygen? +

No. Breathed-out air contains less oxygen and more carbon dioxide than breathed-in air, but it still contains some oxygen. Only part of the oxygen in the air passes into the blood.
