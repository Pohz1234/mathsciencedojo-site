PSLE Science · P5–P6 · Matter and water

Evaporation vs condensation in PSLE Science: the keywords that earn the marks.

Liquid to gas, or gas to liquid? Gains heat, or loses heat? Here is how to tell the two apart and write them the way markers expect.

Evaporation is when liquid water gains heat and changes into water vapour, and condensation is when water vapour loses heat and changes back into liquid water. If your child mixes these up, the usual cause is learning them as two separate definitions instead of as one pair going in opposite directions. Once they see that, the keywords become easy to use correctly.

Evaporation and condensation are opposites, so check the direction of the change

Ask two questions: what state was the water in at the start, and what state is it in at the end? Liquid to gas is evaporation. Gas to liquid is condensation. The gas is water vapour, which is invisible. The white "smoke" you see above a hot drink is tiny droplets of liquid water, which means condensation has already happened.

Evaporation: the water gains heat

Wet clothes dry, puddles shrink and the water level in an uncovered beaker goes down because of evaporation. The heat comes from the surroundings, such as the sun, a warm room or a hot surface. In an answer, the safe wording is that the water gains heat and changes from liquid to water vapour. A phrase like "the water disappeared" or "the water dried up" does not name the process.

Condensation: the water vapour loses heat

Water droplets on the outside of a glass of iced water, mist on a bathroom mirror and droplets under a lid after cooking are all condensation. Here the water vapour in the air touches a cooler surface, loses heat and changes into liquid water. The cooler surface is the important detail. If the question mentions a cold object, the marker is usually looking for it.

Three mistakes that cost marks

  • Saying the water seeped through the glass. The droplets on a cold glass come from water vapour in the air, not from the liquid inside.
  • Using "boiling" for everything. Water can evaporate without boiling. Boiling is a separate, faster process.
  • Forgetting the heat direction. Evaporation: gains heat. Condensation: loses heat. Writing only the name of the process often leaves one mark unclaimed.

Example: the cold glass of water

Question (2 marks): Ali put ice cubes into a glass of water. After a few minutes, water droplets appeared on the outside of the glass. Explain how the water droplets formed.

Model answer: The water vapour in the air touched the cold surface of the glass. The water vapour lost heat and changed into liquid water. This process is called condensation.

The three ideas are there: where the vapour came from, the heat loss, and the keyword. Compare this with the common weaker answer, "the water from inside the glass came out", which earns nothing because it gives the wrong source.

A quick home check

Give your child three everyday situations: wet laundry drying, a mirror fogging up after a shower, and a puddle shrinking in the sun. Ask them to name the process, the state change and whether heat is gained or lost. Doing this out loud for five minutes is more useful than copying definitions again.

Evaporation and condensation: common questions

What is the difference between evaporation and condensation? +

Evaporation is the change of state from liquid to gas (water vapour), which happens when water gains heat. Condensation is the change from gas to liquid, which happens when water vapour loses heat.

Is boiling the same as evaporation? +

No. Evaporation happens at the surface of a liquid at any temperature. Boiling happens throughout the liquid at its boiling point. For PSLE, use evaporation when water slowly disappears from an open container, such as wet clothes drying.

Where do the water droplets on the outside of a cold glass come from? +

They come from the water vapour in the air, not from inside the glass. The water vapour touches the cold surface, loses heat and condenses into liquid water. Many children lose marks by writing that water leaked through the glass.

What makes evaporation faster? +

A higher temperature (more heat gained), a larger exposed surface area, and moving air all speed it up. Always link the factor to the situation in the question.
