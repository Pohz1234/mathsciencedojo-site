Plant transport system in PSLE Science: xylem, phloem and how to answer.

Which tube carries what, and in which direction? Here is how to keep xylem and phloem straight and write answers that earn the marks.

Xylem carries water and mineral salts from the roots up to the leaves, while phloem carries food made in the leaves to the other parts of the plant. If your child mixes the two up, it is usually because they memorised the names without the job and the direction attached. Learn each tube as a short sentence, and the questions become much easier.

Learn each tube as "what it carries and where it goes"

The plant has tubes running through the stem that connect the roots to the leaves. Xylem carries water and mineral salts from the roots to the leaves. Phloem carries food that the leaves have made to the other parts of the plant, such as the stem, roots, flowers and fruits. A one-word answer such as "water" or "food" is incomplete. A full answer names the tube, the substance and the direction.

The roots take in water, the leaves make food

The story starts at the roots, which absorb water and mineral salts from the soil. Root hairs give the root more surface to take in water. The water then moves up the xylem to the leaves. In the leaves, the plant makes food. That food cannot stay in the leaves, because the roots, stem and fruits also need it, so the phloem carries it away. Seeing the two tubes as one delivery system makes it much harder to swap them.

Experiment questions test the xylem, so read the setup carefully

The most common question uses a stalk of celery or a white flower placed in water with red ink or dye. After some time, the leaves or petals change colour. This shows that water travels up the stem. If the stalk is cut across, small coloured dots can be seen, which are the xylem tubes carrying the coloured water.

Example: the celery in red water

Question. A stalk of celery with leaves was placed in a beaker of red water. After a few hours, the leaves had red veins. A cross-section of the stem showed red dots. Explain these observations.

Model answer. The red water was taken up by the celery stalk and carried up through the xylem to the leaves. The xylem carries water and mineral salts from the roots to the leaves, so the dye in the water coloured the veins in the leaves and the xylem tubes in the stem. The red dots in the cross-section are the xylem tubes.

Why it earns the marks. It names the tube (xylem), the substance (water), the direction (up to the leaves), and it links the answer back to the observation (red veins and red dots).

Three habits that keep marks safe

  • Write the substance and the direction together. "Xylem carries water and mineral salts up from the roots to the leaves" covers both in one line.
  • Use the exact words. Say xylem and phloem, not "pipes" or "straws". Say mineral salts, not "nutrients" unless the question uses that word.
  • Link to the experiment. If the question shows coloured water, the answer should mention the colour, where it appeared and why.

Plant transport questions: common questions

What is the difference between xylem and phloem in PSLE Science? +

Xylem carries water and mineral salts from the roots up to the leaves and other parts of the plant. Phloem carries food made in the leaves to the other parts of the plant. In a typical exam answer, naming the substance carried and the direction of travel is what earns the marks.

Why does a white flower change colour when placed in coloured water? +

The coloured water is taken in and carried up through the xylem. As the water reaches the petals, the dye shows up, so the petals change colour. This shows that water moves up the stem through the xylem tubes.

How can my child remember which tube is which? +

Link the first letters to the job. Xylem carries water and minerals up from the roots. Phloem carries the food the leaves have made. A short habit is to write the substance and direction every time, for example: xylem, water and mineral salts, roots to leaves.

Do I need to tell my child the names of every plant part? +

Not every part, but the ones tested should be exact: roots, stem, leaves, and the tubes inside the stem. Marks are usually lost by vague words like pipe or channel, not by missing rare terms.
