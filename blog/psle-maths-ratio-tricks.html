PSLE MATHS · P5–P6 · RATIO

How do I solve PSLE ratio questions with two different ratios?

Make the shared quantity equal in both ratios. Here is the method, a worked example and the checks that save marks.

When a ratio question gives two ratios that share one quantity, or a quantity that stays the same before and after a change, make the units of that shared quantity equal in both ratios. This is the repeated-identity method, and it turns a confusing two-ratio problem into a single bar model you already know how to solve.

Why two ratios cannot be added up directly

Suppose Ali : Ben is 2 : 3 and Ben : Chen is 4 : 5. Ben has 3 units in the first ratio and 4 units in the second. A "unit" in one ratio is not the same size as a "unit" in the other, so you cannot just write 2 : 3 : 4 : 5 or add the numbers. Many students do exactly this, and the whole solution collapses from the first line.

The fix is to make Ben (the repeated identity) have the same number of units in both ratios. Once Ben is equal, the units are the same size and the ratios can be joined.

The four steps of the repeated-identity method

  • 1. Spot the quantity that repeats or the quantity that stays the same.
  • 2. Make its units equal in both ratios. Find a common multiple of the two numbers, then multiply each whole ratio by the right factor.
  • 3. Join the ratios into one, and add up the total units.
  • 4. Find 1 unit from the information given, write what it stands for, and answer the question asked.

Example: two ratios sharing Ben

Ali, Ben and Chen have 175 marbles altogether. The ratio of Ali's marbles to Ben's marbles is 2 : 3. The ratio of Ben's marbles to Chen's marbles is 4 : 5. How many marbles does Chen have?

Ben has 3 units in the first ratio and 4 units in the second. A common multiple of 3 and 4 is 12.
Ali : Ben = 2 : 3 = 8 : 12 (multiply by 4)
Ben : Chen = 4 : 5 = 12 : 15 (multiply by 3)
Ali : Ben : Chen = 8 : 12 : 15
Total units = 8 + 12 + 15 = 35
35 units = 175 marbles
1 unit = 175 ÷ 35 = 5 marbles
Chen: 15 units = 15 × 5 = 75 marbles
Chen has 75 marbles.

Check: Ali 40, Ben 60, Chen 75. 40 : 60 = 2 : 3 and 60 : 75 = 4 : 5. The total is 175.

The same idea works when something stays the same before and after

The repeated identity does not only appear as one person in two ratios. It also appears when a question gives a "before" ratio and an "after" ratio. If Sam spends money but Tom's money does not change, then Tom is the identity. His units must be equal in the before ratio and the after ratio, and only then can you see how many units Sam lost.

The same thinking applies to "the difference stays the same" questions, for example when two people each receive the same amount. There, the difference in units must be equal before and after. Ask one question first: what did not change? That quantity is your anchor. For more on the basic units method, read the PSLE Maths ratio page.

Three habits that protect your marks

  • Write the new ratios with the multiplication shown, such as "× 4" and "× 3". The marker can see your method even if you slip on the arithmetic.
  • Check the answer by putting the numbers back into both original ratios, as in the example above. It takes twenty seconds and catches most errors.
  • Draw the bars when the question is long. A bar model makes the units visible, and it is easier to see which quantity is repeated.

Repeated identity: common questions

What is the repeated identity method in PSLE Maths? +

It is a way of combining two ratios that share one quantity. You make the number of units for that shared quantity the same in both ratios, so the two ratios can be joined into one.

How do I know which quantity to make equal? +

Look for the quantity that appears in both ratios, such as Ben in A : B and B : C. If the question says a quantity stays the same before and after a change, that is the one to match.

Do I need to find the lowest common multiple every time? +

Not always. Any common multiple works, but the lowest common multiple keeps the numbers small, which means fewer chances to slip when you multiply and divide.

What should I write to earn the method marks? +

Show the new ratios with the equal units, write the combined ratio, state what 1 unit stands for, and finish with a sentence answer that includes the unit.
