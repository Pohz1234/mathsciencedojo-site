PSLE Maths careless mistakes: why your child loses marks on sums they already know.

Most "careless" errors are working-presentation habits, not ability. Here is where method marks and accuracy marks are lost, and the routine that wins them back.

"Careless mistake" is the phrase parents hear most after a Maths paper. But when we look at a child's marked script line by line, most of these are not carelessness. They are habits: skipped working, a number copied wrongly, a question half-read, a missing unit. Habits can be trained, which means these marks are the cheapest ones to win back.

The two kinds of marks in PSLE Maths

In structured and long-answer questions, marks are given for two different things:

  • Method marks. Awarded for correct steps and working. If the working is clear and the method is right, the child can still earn marks even when the final answer is wrong.
  • Accuracy marks. Awarded for the correct final answer, including the unit where the question needs one.

A child who does the sum in their head and writes only the answer is gambling everything on one mark. One slip and the whole question is zero. A child who shows the working keeps the method marks even when the arithmetic goes wrong.

Where the marks leak

  • No working. Doing steps mentally and writing only the final answer. Nothing left to give method marks for.
  • Copying errors. Writing 36 as 63, or dropping a digit when moving a number from the question to the working.
  • Answering a different question. Finding the total when the question asks for the difference, or the amount left when it asks for the amount spent.
  • Missing or wrong units. Writing 24 instead of 24 cm2, or $24 when the answer is in cents.
  • Messy layout. Working scattered across the page so the marker cannot follow the steps, and the child cannot check them either.

The 4-step routine we drill in every lesson

  1. Underline the ask. Circle or underline exactly what the question wants (for example "How much more", "in cm") before touching any numbers.
  2. Write the working line by line. One step per line, equals signs lined up, no mental jumps.
  3. Box the answer with its unit. Write a full statement for word problems, such as "Ben has 24 more stickers than Aisha."
  4. Reasonableness check. Ten seconds: does the answer make sense? A shirt that costs $480 or a fraction bigger than the whole should trigger a re-check.

Example: the same wrong arithmetic, two different scores

A child sets up the correct steps for a 3-mark question, then slips on the last subtraction. With every step written, the marker can award the method marks and only the final answer mark is lost. With only a wrong answer on the line, all 3 marks are lost. The maths ability is identical; the habit of showing working is what changed the score.

Careless mistakes: common questions

What are method marks in PSLE Maths? +

Method marks reward the correct steps or working shown in structured and long-answer questions. A child can earn them even when the final answer is wrong, as long as the working is clear and the method is correct.

Why does my child make careless mistakes in PSLE Maths? +

Most careless mistakes are habit problems: skipping the working, copying a number wrongly, misreading what the question asks for, or forgetting the unit. These can be trained out with a fixed routine for writing and checking.

Do I lose marks for forgetting the unit in PSLE Maths? +

Yes. In questions that ask for an answer with a unit, such as cm, kg or $, leaving the unit out can cost the final answer mark even if the number is right.

How can my child reduce careless mistakes before the PSLE? +

Use the same four-step routine on every paper: underline what the question asks for, write the working line by line, box the final answer with its unit, and do a quick reasonableness check before moving on.
