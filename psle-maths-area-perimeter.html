PSLE Maths Area and Perimeter: Composite Figures and Circles

Area is the space inside a figure and perimeter is the distance around it. Most marks are lost in composite figures, when students add a side that is not on the outside edge or forget the curved part.

Area and perimeter in one step chain

  • 1. Label. Write every length you can work out on the figure, including hidden sides.
  • 2. Decide. Ask: is it the space inside (area, cm²) or the distance around (perimeter, cm)?
  • 3. Split or subtract. Cut the figure into rectangles, triangles and circle parts, or take a shape away from a bigger one.
  • 4. Calculate each part on its own line, then add or subtract.
  • 5. Answer with the unit: cm² for area, cm for perimeter.

The formulas to know

  • Rectangle: area = length × breadth. Perimeter = 2 × (length + breadth).
  • Square: area = side × side. Perimeter = 4 × side.
  • Triangle (P5): area = ½ × base × height. The height is at right angles to the base.
  • Circle (P6): circumference = π × diameter. Area = π × radius × radius.
  • Semicircle and quarter circle: take ½ or ¼ of the circle. For perimeter, remember to add the straight edges.
  • The question states the value of π to use, usually 3.14 or 22/7. Use exactly that.

Worked examples

Example 1: triangle with a slanted side

A triangle has a base of 12 cm. Its height is 9 cm and its slanted side is 15 cm. Find its area.

Area = ½ × base × height
= ½ × 12 × 9
= 54
The area is 54 cm². (The 15 cm slanted side is not used for the area.)

Example 2: quarter circle (π = 3.14)

A quarter circle has a radius of 10 cm. Find its area and its perimeter.

Area = ¼ × 3.14 × 10 × 10 = 78.5 cm²
Curved part = ¼ × 2 × 3.14 × 10 = 15.7 cm
Perimeter = 15.7 + 10 + 10 = 35.7
The area is 78.5 cm² and the perimeter is 35.7 cm. (The two radii are part of the boundary, so they are added.)

Tricky question: area and perimeter after a cut-out

Practice question (π = 22/7)

A rectangle is 20 cm long and 14 cm wide. A semicircle is cut out of the rectangle. Its diameter is the whole of one 14 cm side, so the curved edge bulges inwards. Find (a) the area and (b) the perimeter of the figure that is left.

Worked solution
Label: radius of the semicircle = 14 ÷ 2 = 7 cm.
(a) Subtract:
Area of rectangle = 20 × 14 = 280 cm²
Area of semicircle = ½ × 22/7 × 7 × 7 = 77 cm²
Area left = 280 − 77 = 203
The area is 203 cm².
(b) Trace the outside boundary: the two 20 cm sides, the untouched 14 cm side, and the curved edge. The 14 cm side that was cut out is no longer on the boundary.
Curved edge = ½ × 22/7 × 14 = 22 cm
Perimeter = 20 + 20 + 14 + 22 = 76
The perimeter is 76 cm.

Why it is tricky: many students subtract the semicircle from the perimeter, or keep the 14 cm diameter side. The cut-out removes that straight side and adds the curved edge in its place.

Mistakes that cost marks

  • Using the slanted side of a triangle as the height.
  • Adding inside edges, such as the cut line between two shapes, to the perimeter.
  • Using the diameter in the area formula where the radius is needed.
  • Forgetting the straight edges when finding the perimeter of a semicircle or quarter circle.
  • Writing cm for area. Area is in cm², perimeter in cm.
  • Not showing which shapes were added or subtracted, which loses the method mark.

Area and perimeter questions

What is the difference between area and perimeter? +

Area is the amount of surface inside a figure, measured in square units such as cm². Perimeter is the total length of the boundary around it, measured in cm, m or other length units.

What is the formula for the area of a triangle? +

Area = ½ × base × height. The height must be at right angles to the base. A slanted side is not the height.

What are the circle formulas in PSLE Maths? +

Circumference = π × diameter (or 2 × π × radius). Area = π × radius × radius. The question tells you to use 3.14 or 22/7 for π.

How do you find the perimeter of a figure with a curved part? +

Add the straight sides on the outside boundary and the length of the curved part. For a semicircle, the arc is half the circumference. Leave out any straight edge that is inside the figure.

Why does my child lose marks in composite figure questions? +

The usual causes are using a slanted side as the height, adding inside edges to the perimeter, using the diameter as the radius, and not showing which shapes were added or subtracted.
