PSLE Maths Speed: Speed, Distance, Time and Average Speed

Speed tells you how far something travels in a given time. The three formulas are linked, and the most common lost mark is averaging two speeds instead of using total distance and total time.

The three formulas

  • Speed = Distance ÷ Time
  • Distance = Speed × Time
  • Time = Distance ÷ Speed
  • Average speed = Total distance ÷ Total time.
  • Keep the units matching: km with hours gives km/h, and m with seconds gives m/s.

Worked examples

Example 1: find the speed

A car travels 150 km in 2 h 30 min. What is its average speed in km/h?

2 h 30 min = 2.5 h
Speed = 150 ÷ 2.5 = 60
The average speed is 60 km/h.

Example 2: average speed

A man cycles 60 km at 60 km/h and then 60 km at 30 km/h. What is his average speed for the whole journey?

Time for first part = 60 ÷ 60 = 1 h
Time for second part = 60 ÷ 30 = 2 h
Total distance = 120 km, total time = 3 h
Average speed = 120 ÷ 3 = 40
The average speed is 40 km/h. (Not 45 km/h, which is the average of the two speeds.)

Mistakes that cost marks

  • Averaging the two speeds instead of dividing total distance by total time.
  • Writing 2 h 30 min as 2.30 h. It is 2.5 h.
  • Mixing units, such as km with minutes, without converting.
  • Leaving out the unit, such as km/h, in the final answer.

Speed questions

What is the formula for speed in PSLE Maths? +

Speed = Distance ÷ Time. From it, Distance = Speed × Time and Time = Distance ÷ Speed.

How do you find average speed? +

Divide the total distance by the total time for the whole journey. Do not add the speeds and divide by 2, unless the times are equal.

How do you convert minutes to hours in speed questions? +

Divide the minutes by 60. 30 minutes is 0.5 h, and 2 h 30 min is 2.5 h.

Why does my child lose marks in speed problem sums? +

The common causes are averaging the speeds, writing time wrongly, mixing units, and not showing the working line by line.
