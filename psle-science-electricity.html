PSLE Science Electricity: Circuits, Conductors and Series vs Parallel Explained

A bulb lights up only if there is a complete path with no gaps. Most marks in this topic are lost by not saying the circuit is closed, or by mixing up what happens in series and in parallel.

A bulb lights up only when the circuit is closed

Remember it as a step chain: battery → wire → bulb → wire → back to the battery. If every link is joined and the switch is closed, the circuit is a closed circuit and electricity flows round it. Break any link and it becomes an open circuit: nothing flows and the bulb does not light.

  • Closed circuit: a complete path with no gaps. The bulb lights up.
  • Open circuit: there is a gap (an open switch, a loose wire or a missing part). The bulb does not light up.
  • Switch: opens or closes the circuit to turn the bulb off or on.
  • Short circuit: a wire joins the two ends of the bulb directly, so the bulb is bypassed and does not light.

Conductors let electricity pass; insulators do not

To test a material, place it in a gap in a working circuit. If the bulb lights, the material is an electrical conductor. If the bulb stays off, it is an electrical insulator.

  • Conductors: metals such as copper, iron, aluminium and steel.
  • Insulators: plastic, rubber, wood, glass and cloth.
  • Wires are made of metal (conductor) inside a plastic cover (insulator) so that the electricity stays in the wire and users stay safe.

Series: one path. Parallel: more than one path.

Step chain for series: one path only → remove or fuse one bulb → the circuit becomes open → all the bulbs go off. Step chain for parallel: separate paths → remove one bulb → only that path is open → the bulbs on the other paths stay lit.

  • More batteries joined in series in the same circuit make the bulb brighter.
  • More bulbs joined in series with the same battery make each bulb dimmer.
  • A part that lies on the one shared path (for example the main wire or a switch next to the battery) controls every bulb that depends on it.

Common student mistakes

  • Writing "the bulb is spoilt" when the question asks about an open circuit. First check for a gap in the circuit.
  • Not saying the circuit is closed (or open). This is the keyword examiners look for.
  • Believing the metal parts of a bulb do not matter. The bulb must be connected at its metal base and its metal side, otherwise the circuit is not complete.
  • Saying a material is a conductor because it is "strong" or "hard". Conductors are decided by testing in a circuit, not by how a material feels.
  • Forgetting that in a parallel circuit a bulb on one path can go off while the bulbs on other paths stay lit.

Tricky practice question with full worked solution

Bulbs X, Y and Z

Three identical bulbs are connected to one battery and a switch. Bulb Z is on the main path next to the battery. Bulbs X and Y are on two separate paths that branch off after Z and rejoin before the battery. The switch is closed and all three bulbs are lit.

(a) Bulb X fuses. State and explain what happens to bulbs Y and Z.

(b) Bulb Z fuses instead. State and explain what happens to bulbs X and Y.

Solution.

(a) Bulbs Y and Z stay lit. Bulb X is on its own separate path, so when it fuses only that path becomes open. The path through Z and Y is still a closed circuit, so electricity still flows through them.

(b) Bulbs X and Y both go off. Bulb Z is on the main path that the electricity must pass through to reach both X and Y. When Z fuses, the main path becomes open, so there is no closed circuit for any bulb and electricity cannot flow.

Why this is tricky: students see "parallel" and write that the other bulbs stay lit in both parts. Always ask: is the broken part on its own path, or on the shared path?

Electricity questions

What is a closed circuit in PSLE Science? +

A closed circuit is a complete path with no gaps, made of the battery, wires, bulb and a closed switch, all joined to one another. The bulb lights up only when the circuit is closed.

Why does a bulb not light up even though it is connected to a battery? +

The most common reasons are: there is a gap in the circuit (open circuit or an open switch), a part in the path is made of an insulator, the bulb is fused, or the battery has run out.

What is the difference between a series and a parallel circuit? +

In a series circuit there is only one path, so if one bulb is removed or fused, all the bulbs go off. In a parallel circuit the bulbs are on separate paths, so if one bulb is removed, the bulbs on the other paths stay lit.

What is the difference between an electrical conductor and an insulator? +

An electrical conductor allows electricity to pass through it, for example metals such as copper, iron and aluminium. An electrical insulator does not allow electricity to pass through it, for example plastic, rubber, wood and glass.
