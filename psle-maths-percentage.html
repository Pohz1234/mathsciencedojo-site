PSLE Maths Percentage: Percentage of a Quantity, Discount and Percentage Change

Percentage means out of 100. Most percentage marks are lost by using the wrong base, so always decide first which amount is 100%.

Three things to do first

  • Find the base. The amount you compare against is 100%. For a discount or an increase, it is the original amount.
  • Percentage of a quantity. 15% of 200 = 15 ÷ 100 × 200 = 30.
  • Percentage change = change ÷ original × 100%. From 40 to 50, the change is 10, and 10 ÷ 40 × 100% = 25%.

Worked examples

Example 1: discount

A bag costs $80. It is sold at a 25% discount. What is the new price?

Discount = 25% of $80 = $20
New price = $80 − $20 = $60
The new price is $60. (Or: 75% of $80 = $60.)

Example 2: find the original

After a 20% discount, a pair of shoes costs $48. What was the original price?

Original = 100%. After discount = 100% − 20% = 80%.
80% = $48
1% = $48 ÷ 80 = $0.60
100% = 100 × $0.60 = $60
The original price was $60.

Mistakes that cost marks

  • Taking the discount off the new price instead of the original price.
  • Working out 20% of $48 when the $48 is already 80% of the original.
  • Dividing the change by the new amount instead of the original amount in percentage change.
  • Forgetting the % sign or the $ unit in the final answer.

Percentage questions

What does percentage mean in PSLE Maths? +

Percentage means out of 100. 25% means 25 out of 100, which is the same as the fraction 25/100 or 1/4.

How do you find the percentage change? +

Subtract to find the change, divide by the original amount, then multiply by 100%. From 40 to 50, the change is 10, and 10 ÷ 40 × 100% = 25%.

How do you find the original price after a discount? +

Work out what percentage the sale price is of the original. After a 20% discount, the price is 80% of the original, so divide the sale price by 80 and multiply by 100.

Why is the base important in percentage questions? +

The base is the amount that stands for 100%. Using the wrong base, such as the new price instead of the original price, gives the wrong answer even when the working looks correct.
